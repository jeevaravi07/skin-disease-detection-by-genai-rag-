DISEASE: Atopic dermatitis
WHAT IT IS: Atopic dermatitis (atopic eczema) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition associated with skin-barrier dysfunction. It is not contagious. A photo alone cannot confirm it.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: Dry, itchy, inflamed skin; recurrent flares; rough, cracked, thickened or discoloured patches. Appearance varies by age, site and skin tone.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Interacting genetic, immune and skin-barrier factors. Heat, sweat, fragrances, harsh soaps and some fabrics may worsen symptoms. It is not necessarily caused by food allergy.
GENERAL CARE: Use gentle fragrance-free cleanser, lukewarm water and fragrance-free moisturiser regularly. Keep nails short, avoid scratching, and follow a clinician's care plan.
WHAT TO AVOID: Known triggers, harsh/fragranced products and very hot showers. Do not start restrictive diets or stop prescribed treatment without medical advice.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Moisturising routine; whether prescribed topical anti-inflammatory treatment is appropriate; specialist options for persistent/severe disease; assessment for infection if worsening.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Arrange assessment for persistent, recurrent or worsening rash. Seek prompt care for pain, warmth, swelling, pus, fever or rapidly worsening symptoms. Emergency care for breathing difficulty or facial/throat swelling.
LIMITATION: Educational information only, not diagnosis or personalised treatment.
