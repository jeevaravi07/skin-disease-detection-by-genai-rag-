DISEASE: Seborrheic dermatitis
WHAT IT IS: A common inflammatory condition affecting oily areas, especially scalp and face. Dandruff is often a mild scalp presentation. It is not contagious and may recur.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: White/yellowish, dry or greasy scale; itch or inflamed patches on scalp, eyebrows, sides of nose, ears, beard area or chest. Appearance varies by skin tone.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Multifactorial; associated with the skin's response to Malassezia yeast, skin oils and inflammation. Stress, cold/dry weather and fatigue may correlate with flares.
GENERAL CARE: Gentle cleansing; do not pick or scrape scale. Ask a pharmacist/clinician about suitable anti-dandruff shampoo or other products. Follow labels and avoid eye contact.
WHAT TO AVOID: Harsh scrubbing, irritating fragrances and products that worsen skin. Do not use scalp products on sensitive areas unless label/clinician says appropriate. Avoid prolonged steroid use without advice.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Appropriate medicated shampoo/topical antifungal depending on site and age; short-term anti-inflammatory treatment when appropriate; evaluation if severe or not improving.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Assessment if persistent, extensive, painful or not improving. Prompt care for pus, marked swelling, increasing pain or suspected infection; urgent assessment for rapidly worsening symptoms or significant eye-area inflammation.
LIMITATION: Scalp flaking and facial rashes have several causes; clinician assessment may be needed.
