DISEASE: Scabies
WHAT IT IS: An infestation by the human itch mite. It commonly spreads through prolonged direct skin contact and among close contacts. It is not caused by poor hygiene. A photo cannot confirm it.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: Intense itching, often worse at night; small itchy bumps and sometimes tiny burrow-like lines. Common sites include finger webs, wrists, elbows, armpits, waist, buttocks and genital areas. Symptoms may take weeks to appear after first infestation.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Sarcoptes scabiei mites; close contact and shared living arrangements facilitate spread. Crusted scabies is a severe, highly contagious form.
GENERAL CARE: Contact a healthcare professional if suspected; prescription treatment is generally needed. Follow directions exactly. Household/close contacts may need coordinated treatment. Wash recently used bedding/clothes/towels hot when fabric allows; seek local guidance for items that cannot be washed.
WHAT TO AVOID: Do not rely on ordinary OTC itch products to kill mites. Never apply insecticide sprays, fumigants, household pesticides or veterinary products to skin. Avoid sharing personal items while managing infestation.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Prescribed scabicide cream/lotion or oral medicine when appropriate; application instructions, repeat treatment and contact management; safe options for infants/pregnancy/other conditions.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Contact a clinician if suspected. Prompt care for sores, pus, spreading warmth/redness or fever. Reassessment may be needed for new burrows/rash or persistent itching after treatment.
LIMITATION: Symptoms overlap with eczema and other rashes. Do not self-treat based on an AI prediction.
