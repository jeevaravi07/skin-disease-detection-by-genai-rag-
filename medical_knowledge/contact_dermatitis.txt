DISEASE: Contact dermatitis
WHAT IT IS: Skin inflammation caused by direct contact with an irritant or allergen. Irritant dermatitis damages/irritates skin; allergic dermatitis is an immune reaction. It is not contagious.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: Itching, burning, stinging, redness or other colour change, dryness, scaling, cracking, swelling and sometimes blisters. Often occurs where contact happened; timing varies.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Detergents, soaps, cleaning products, solvents, repeated wet work, fragrances, nickel, latex, preservatives, cosmetics and plants. Repeated occupational exposure may contribute.
GENERAL CARE: Stop a suspected new product, gently rinse exposure when appropriate, use bland fragrance-free moisturiser, track exposures and seek advice about patch testing for recurring cases.
WHAT TO AVOID: Suspected irritants/allergens, harsh cleansers, fragranced products and home remedies that worsen the rash. Avoid unsupervised strong prescription creams.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Identify and avoid cause; barrier protection; appropriate moisturiser or prescribed topical medicine; patch testing or occupational-health advice.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Assessment if unclear, persistent, recurring, spreading, or affecting face/eyes/genitals. Prompt care for extensive blistering, severe swelling, pain, pus or fever. Emergency help for breathing difficulty or tongue/throat swelling.
LIMITATION: A photo cannot reliably distinguish allergic from irritant dermatitis or other rashes.
