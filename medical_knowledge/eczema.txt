DISEASE: Eczema (general category)
WHAT IT IS: Eczema describes a group of conditions causing inflamed, irritated, often itchy skin. Atopic dermatitis is one type; contact dermatitis and other forms differ. “Eczema” alone does not specify a subtype.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: Itch, dryness, scaling, inflamed patches, cracks and sometimes blisters or oozing. Symptoms vary by subtype, age, site and skin tone.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Depend on subtype; may include skin-barrier dysfunction, inflammation, irritants or allergic reactions. Heat, sweat, frequent washing, fragrance and friction can worsen symptoms in some people.
GENERAL CARE: Gentle fragrance-free cleanser/moisturiser, lukewarm water, reduce friction and scratching, track triggers, and follow a clinician's care plan.
WHAT TO AVOID: Products that reliably irritate skin; unsupervised strong steroid, antibiotic, antifungal or combination creams; assuming every rash called eczema needs the same treatment.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Confirm subtype and rule out infection/other disease; moisturisers, trigger management and appropriate prescribed treatment; specialist care for severe or unresponsive disease.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Assessment for first-time, persistent, worsening or recurring rash. Prompt care for pain, warmth, swelling, pus, fever or painful blisters; urgent assessment for severe symptoms or eye involvement.
LIMITATION: General educational information only; eczema is not one precise diagnosis.
