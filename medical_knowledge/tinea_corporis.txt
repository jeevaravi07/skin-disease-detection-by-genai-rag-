DISEASE: Tinea corporis (ringworm of the body)
WHAT IT IS: A superficial fungal skin infection, not a worm. It may spread through infected people, animals, objects and sometimes contaminated soil.
COMMON SYMPTOMS: An itchy expanding round/ring-shaped patch may have a raised scaly edge and clearer centre. It may not form a classic ring. Other conditions can look similar.
POSSIBLE CAUSES / CONTRIBUTING FACTORS: Dermatophyte fungi infect outer skin layers. Spread can occur via skin contact, infected pets, shared towels/clothes, sports equipment or warm damp environments. Sweating and close-contact sports increase exposure.
GENERAL CARE: Keep area clean and dry, wash hands after touching it, do not share towels/clothes/razors/equipment, launder items contacting the rash, and ask a pharmacist/clinician about appropriate antifungal treatment.
WHAT TO AVOID: Scratching and sharing personal items. Do not use steroid-only creams on suspected fungal rash unless specifically advised; steroids can mask/worsen fungal infection. Do not diagnose by ring shape alone.
TREATMENT TO DISCUSS WITH A CLINICIAN: Appropriate topical antifungal for limited infection; whether testing or oral treatment is needed for extensive/recurrent disease. Scalp, beard, nail or other sites may need different care.
WHEN TO SEEK HELP: Assessment if extensive, worsening, recurrent, near eyes or not improving; seek advice for immunocompromised people. Prompt care for marked pain, pus, fever or rapidly spreading inflammation.
LIMITATION: Photo classification cannot confirm fungal infection; treatment depends on site and clinical diagnosis.
